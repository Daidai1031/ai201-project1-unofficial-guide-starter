## Product Studio Pre-Work Assignments

In addition to individual quizzes, teams were asked to work on several main assignments for pre-work:

- Develop an Interview Guide
- Complete an Industry Research Summary Deck
- Conduct 10 Interviews

## Develop an Interview Guide

Teams were advised to avoid the "Bob's Treadmill" problem.

Students were told not to frame questions by asking people to predict what they want in a solution. Instead, teams should use interviews to understand people's current workflows, processes, and thoughts.

The announcement emphasized that teams should understand the problem, not the solution.

## Industry Research Summary Deck

Teams were asked to conduct industry research and complete a rich picture.

The announcement noted that a team's "How Might We" question may not fit neatly within one industry. This is acceptable and expected. Teams may need to research more than one industry to fully understand the problem space.

Teams were encouraged to research the patchwork of players involved, how those players relate to one another, and how they are involved in the current problem.

Important research questions included:

- What is the status quo?
- Why does this problem exist?
- Who are the players involved?
- How are different players connected?
- What role does the company play in the broader problem space?

Students who were unsure where to start were encouraged to sign up for office hours with the library team.

Teams were reminded that their company should be understood as one player in the ecosystem, not as the center of the research. The company should not be placed at the center of the rich picture.

The announcement emphasized that the goal of the class is not simply to design something that serves the company alone. The company is invested in solving the problem, but it is not the client. Focusing only on the company may cause teams to miss important elements of the broader problem.

## Conduct 10 Interviews

Teams were asked to conduct 10 interviews as a group.

The announcement noted that company advisors may be valuable resources for connecting teams to future stakeholders, but students were also encouraged to show entrepreneurial initiative and find interviewees on their own.

Teams were advised to start reaching out early and creatively consider how to "fish where the fish are."

Examples from past teams included:

- Students who needed to talk to cab drivers spent extra time at JFK speaking with cab drivers waiting for passengers.
- Students who needed to talk to expecting mothers joined Facebook communities for expecting moms to schedule interviews.
- Students researching internal hospital logistics initially tried to reach hospital leaders, but later realized nurses were more accessible and often knew just as much about how systems actually worked.

The hospital logistics example emphasized that changing perspective on who might be important to interview can improve access and lead to a more useful solution. Many solutions rely on frontline staff, such as nurses, to actually implement them.

## Spring Studio Kickoff

The Spring Studio Kickoff introduced the Spring Studio track options and the assignments students needed to complete for Spring teaming.

Students were encouraged to use available time around the kickoff to complete their team's retrospective.

## Team Reflection Before Track Selection

Before continuing toward Spring Studio, teams were asked to reflect on the three ideas they currently had and the feedback they had received.

Teams were asked to determine which two ideas remained the most viable and should continue forward.

## Derisking

A Derisking session was scheduled so students could ask questions about derisking assignments before completing them.

This indicates that Product Studio expected teams to identify risks in their ideas and design ways to test those risks before committing fully to a direction.

## Maker Day Feedback and Project Direction

After Maker Day 2, teams were encouraged to reflect on verbal feedback from coaches while waiting for written feedback.

Students were told to start thinking about next steps for their projects and prepare for Maker Day 3.

Maker Day feedback was framed as a way to help teams decide which venture had more confidence, which experiments were stronger, and which ideas gained the most traction with coaches.

## Spring Studio Teaming Events

Studio hosted teaming events to help students find and solidify teams for the Spring Studio tracks.

These events were connected to the transition from Product Studio into Spring Studio tracks.

## Designing Experiments

A session on designing experiments was scheduled to help teams design stronger experiments for their projects.

Teams were expected to use feedback and research to decide which experiments they would run over the upcoming month.

## Preparing for Crit #2

Before Crit #2, teams were expected to come with a provisional, not final, decision about which venture they would finish the semester with.

Teams were advised to:

- Revisit verbal feedback from Maker Day 2
- Identify which venture had more team confidence
- Identify which experiments were more solid
- Notice which ideas gained the most traction with coaches
- Decide which experiments to run over the upcoming month
- Recruit participants for experiments
- Improve experiment design before receiving more feedback

The goal was for teams to come to Crit #2 and the Company Advisor Meeting ready to receive useful feedback on their provisional choice.
