# [Cornell Tech] Why I enrolled in Startup Studio at Cornell Tech this semester

Author: Hwee Lin Yeo, Alexandra

Published: Jan 26, 2020

Last August, I started my M.Eng in CS graduate program at Cornell Tech right after graduating from Cornell University. For me, it was a dream come true — I get to be in the city (bye Upstate New York!), stay in school in another year (since I graduated a year early too), and most important of all, I get to take classes that I’ve always wanted to take even back in undergrad. I know, that’s such a nerdy thing to say, but learning about blockchains, network security and work on a startup? Sign me up!

For Product Studio in Fall semester, I worked with Estee Lauder with my teammates on a holistic skincare diary to improve customers’ post-purchase experience: customers could input their daily habits, products used and location to better understand their skin health. We demo-ed our app, presented numerous times in critique sessions and in Estee Lauder, and received great feedback overall.

This semester, we have the choice of either BigCo studio, which meant that we get to work with a big company, or Startup Studio, where we could build our own working product. I chose the latter, to get my hands dirty and experience building things from ground-up.

Together with my new teammates Fabio (an MBA), Ryan (fellow CS student) and Zhen Wei (a Connective Media student), we set out to find ideas to work on in the consumer space — it’s fun, iterable and has fewer restrictions/regulations. Moreover, the positive experience I had during Product Studio also made me really excited to work on more consumer products. We soon find it to be not quite straightforward, since many great things have been done already.

We first wanted to create a collaborative travel planning platform for millennials to plan getaways, negotiate their travel budget and voice out how they feel about different activities/places, but our advisors thought the travel industry is hard to break through. Moreover, Scout Explore appeared in Product Hunt and I really liked using it! We also explored the idea of a AI-powered smart assistant for students, given how much information channels students have to tune in to.

It seems like a possible idea, but it’s also hard to differentiate from other smart assistants.

We wanted to continue working in the consumer space and brainstormed on applying viable business models into different industries. And there, we thought of ClassPass, for couples date activities. See, going out regularly as a couple can get expensive easily — eating out at restaurants, going for a session of mini-golfing or kayaking would quickly add up and burn a hole in the wallet.

But some couples just wanna head out! We thought the ClassPass model was viable in this context, since frequency and variety of activities would be desired by our target users, like in the case of ClassPass and KidPass.

Similar to the ClassPass model, we envision differently tiered memberships that give users a given number of points to be used for the events they desire. We will include features such as machine learning-powered activity recommendations and calendar syncing (especially needed for working couples) with a ranking of activities to make it easier for couples to decide on what they want to do.

Now go forth on your dates!

HowAboutWe did offer a similar subscription service, except that it targets people who use dating apps — and thus, not couples who would head out together regularly. Last week, we received our first feedback from David Tish, an investor at Box Group, an NYC based venture capital firm. He described it as a “graveyard” idea, but our group would let our customer and product discovery processes guide us in tweaks or pivots.

So yes, we’ll keep working. Feel free to reach out (inbox or comment) if you have any insight for us. Any feedback would help!
